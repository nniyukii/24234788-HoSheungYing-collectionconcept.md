The subject is a glass of soda water transitioning from a lively, carbonated state into flat water. I chose soda water as the subject because its decay is fast, visible, and completely irreversible within a single sitting, unlike other subjects that take days or months to change. This collection shows an irreversible loss of energy and effervescence, shedding its bubbles, carbonation bite, and sound for a glass of soda water over time. Anyone could look at this glass of water, but only those who watch it until the last sip would notice the specific loss of its life.
All 20 records focus on the same glass and follow the same timeline of decay. Under the heading "Quietude in a Glass," the collection argues that loss is an unstoppable slowdown, leaving the empty glass behind while erasing its active energy. If someone re-sorted the records by file type, they would lose the linear story of decay, turning a narrative of loss into a random catalog. Without the heading, the records are just scattered files. Besides, talking about the odd one out, it is the final record showing completely flat, still water with total silence. I kept it in because without the end point of total death and silence, the story of decay would feel incomplete. I think this shows a clear ending of the collection.
However, the collection cannot capture the physical feelings. For example, the sharp sting of bubbles on the tongue, the cold chill on the fingers when holding the glass, or the exact pressure of a popping bubble. These missing parts are marked in the not_captured fields, showing where digital data fails to record real physical experience.
